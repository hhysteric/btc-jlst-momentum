Paper Core: How Reversal and Momentum Coexist

▼

The JLST Model (Jegadeesh, Luo, Subrahmanyam & Titman, 2025) provides a unified explanation for two seemingly contradictory patterns in asset returns:

1. Short-term reversals (Table 2: ρ₁ < 0): Past 1-month returns negatively predict future returns. This arises because noise traders (retail investors) create temporary price deviations from fundamentals. Risk-averse market makers demand a premium to absorb these noise demands, causing prices to overshoot and then revert.

rt = α + ρ₁·rt−1 + ρ₂·rt−2 + … + ρ₁₂·rt−12 + εt   where ρ₁ < 0

2. Longer-term momentum (Table 2: ρ₃…ρ₁₂ > 0): Returns at lags 3–12 months are positively predictive. This stems from active investors' underreaction to information they did not produce (a form of overconfidence per Odean 1998). Prices gradually incorporate fundamental information, creating momentum.

3. The transition (Table 2: ρ₂ ≈ 0): The 2-month lag coefficient is insignificant — a dead zone where reversal forces and momentum forces roughly cancel out.

Proposition 2 (Skip effect): Skipping one period between formation and holding periods enhances momentum: L* = L − Cov(P₂−P₀, P₃−P₂) > L. This is because skipping removes the negative covariance caused by noise-trader-induced reversals.

BTC Application: BTC's high retail participation and leverage amplify the noise trading channel, predicting stronger reversals than in traditional equities. The 24/7 market and extreme tail events require adapted parameters (30-day months, log returns, shorter normalization windows).

Prediction (a): Information Shocks Attenuate Reversals

▼

Paper (Proposition 3): After sufficiently precise public signals (earnings announcements), short-term reversals attenuate. The underreaction to the signal creates post-announcement drift that more than offsets noise-trader-induced reversals.

BTC Adaptation: BTC has no earnings announcements. We substitute information shocks: events where |log return| > 2.5× ATR% AND volume > 2× average volume. Examples: ETF approvals, regulatory news, exchange collapses.

Implementation: Within 10 bars after a shock, the reversal component (Rev) is multiplied by 0.40. This means: don't rely on "oversold bounce" logic right after a major information event — the market is absorbing genuine news, not just noise.

Empirical support: Table 3 of the paper shows reversals attenuate by ~68% in the month after earnings announcements (c = +0.025 vs ρ₁ = −0.037).

Prediction (b): Reversal and Momentum Profits Are Negatively Correlated

▼

Paper: Because exogenous shifts in noise trading generate stronger reversals and weaker momentum, the model predicts an inverse relation between reversal and momentum profits — across countries and across time.

Implementation: We compute rolling corr(Rev, Mom) over 180 bars. Normally this should be negative (healthy complementary regime, shown as blue background). When it turns significantly positive (> 0.20), the two effects are resonating — this is anomalous and typically indicates crowded trades or a regime shift.

Action: When corr > threshold, the composite score is automatically discounted by the penalty factor (0.60). The background turns orange as a warning. This is a risk switch, not a timing signal.

Prediction (c): More Noise Trading → Stronger Reversals

▼

Paper (Proposition 1): As the scale of noise trades (νz) increases, short-term reversals strengthen (S becomes more negative) and momentum attenuates (L decreases).

Noise proxy for BTC: The paper uses institutional ownership and retail order imbalance. For BTC, we use: ATR% level (40%), volume anomaly (30%), and volatility regime — ATR fast/slow ratio (30%). These are combined into a z-scored noise proxy.

Dynamic weighting:

wRev = max(0, 0.50 × (1 + 0.50 × noiseZ))
wMom = max(0, 0.50 × (1 − 0.50 × noiseZ × 0.5))
→ normalized so wRev + wMom = 1

When noise is high → reversal weight increases, momentum weight decreases. BTC's default noise sensitivity (0.50) is higher than equities (0.40) because of its much higher retail/leverage participation.

Key insight for BTC: The paper predicts BTC should exhibit stronger reversals than equities due to its noise-dominated market structure. Monitor AC(1) — if it's consistently negative, this prediction holds.

Limitations & Caveats (Must Read)

▼

1. Cross-section vs. time-series: The paper's profits come from cross-sectional decile hedge portfolios (long winners, short losers). This dashboard is a single-asset time-series approximation — not statistically equivalent. Use as a directional bias/filter, not standalone alpha.

2. BTC regime non-stationarity: BTC's market structure has changed dramatically — from retail-only (2017) to futures (2018), to institutional + spot ETF (2024). Parameters calibrated on earlier data may not apply post-2024.

3. Short sample: Only ~7 years of reliable BTCUSDT data, <4 complete cycles. All statistical estimates carry wide confidence intervals.

4. Halving module is narrative, not statistical: Only 4 historical halvings, confounded with macro events. Treat as visual reference only.

5. Missing funding rate: The single biggest improvement would be incorporating perpetual funding rates as a noise proxy — it's more direct than ATR/volume. Not available in standard Binance kline data.

6. No risk management: This is an indicator, not a strategy. It contains no stop-loss, position sizing, or portfolio construction logic.

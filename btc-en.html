v2 BTC-Adapted Version Notes

▼

Why v2? v1 directly uses stock market parameters from the paper (monthly-scale windows, institutional holdings as noise proxy). BTC trades 24/7 with high volatility and a market structure dominated by retail and leveraged participants, fundamentally different from traditional equities. v2 calibrates optimal parameters through backtesting on BTC historical data.

1. Backtested parameters: Grid search on 2017-2023 BTC daily data, optimizing reversal window, momentum window, skip period, normalization window, entry threshold, and noise sensitivity using "signal direction hit rate x risk-adjusted return (Sharpe)" as the objective function. 2024-2026 data serves as out-of-sample validation.

2. Funding rate noise proxy: The paper uses institutional holdings ratio / retail fund flows to measure noise trader density. The most direct BTC substitute is the perpetual contract funding rate:

v2 noise_z = 0.25×ATR + 0.25×Vol Ratio + 0.20×Regime + 0.30×|Funding Rate|_z

High funding rate → high leverage/speculation → high noise → higher reversal weight. This more directly reflects "noise trader" density than pure ATR.

3. Lower entry threshold: Backtesting shows BTC benefits from a lower entry threshold (0.5 vs 1.0) to capture more valid signals, since the combined reversal-momentum force in BTC often does not reach the paper's default ±1.0 extreme values.

4. Lower noise sensitivity: v2's noiseGain (0.3 vs 0.5) is more conservative, avoiding excessive weight adjustment in BTC's inherently high-volatility environment.

⚠️ Overfitting warning: Backtested optimization carries overfitting risk. Out-of-sample (2024+) performance may be weaker than the training period. v2 parameters should be treated as "a more reasonable initial guess for BTC market structure," not a guaranteed profitable trading system.

Paper Core: How Reversals and Momentum Coexist

▼

The JLST Model (Jegadeesh, Luo, Subrahmanyam & Titman, 2025) provides a unified explanation for two seemingly contradictory patterns in asset returns:

1. Short-term reversal (Table 2: ρ₁ < 0): Past 1-month returns are negatively correlated with future returns. This occurs because noise traders cause prices to temporarily deviate from fundamentals.

rt = α + ρ₁·rt−1 + ρ₂·rt−2 + … + ρ₁₂·rt−12 + εt   where ρ₁ < 0

2. Medium-to-long-term momentum (Table 2: ρ₃…ρ₁₂ > 0): Returns lagged 3-12 months have positive predictive power. This stems from informed investors' underreaction to information.

3. Transition zone (Table 2: ρ₂ ≈ 0): The regression coefficient at lag 2 is insignificant — a dead zone where reversal and momentum forces roughly cancel out.

Limitations & Caveats (Must Read)

▼

1. Cross-section vs time series: The paper studies cross-sectional decile hedge portfolios. This dashboard is a single-asset time-series approximation — statistically not equivalent.

2. Overfitting risk: v2 parameters are optimized through backtesting, with potential overfitting to the training period. Monitor out-of-sample performance.

3. Short sample: Only about 9 years of reliable data, during which market structure has changed multiple times (pure retail → futures → institutional ETFs).

4. Funding rate data: Only available from 2019-09 onward. Earlier calculations do not include this factor. Cached data is used when API access is limited.

5. No risk management: This is an indicator, not a trading system. It does not include stop-losses, position sizing, or portfolio logic.

Theory basis: N. Jegadeesh, J. Luo, A. Subrahmanyam, S. Titman (2025), "Short-Term Reversals and Longer-Term Momentum around the World," Review of Financial Studies.
